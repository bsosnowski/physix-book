# Gravitation & Orbits

Newton's great unification: the force that drops an apple is the force
that holds the Moon.

## The inverse-square law

Every pair of masses attracts along the line joining them:

$$
F = G\frac{Mm}{r^2}
$$

For a small body orbiting a much heavier one, only the combination
<M v="mu">\mu = GM</M> matters. The specific energy (per unit mass) and
specific angular momentum are both conserved:

$$
\varepsilon = \frac{v^2}{2} - \frac{\mu}{r}, \qquad
h = r\,v_\perp
$$

The sign of $\varepsilon$ classifies the trajectory once and for all:
$\varepsilon < 0$ — bound ellipse; $\varepsilon = 0$ — parabolic escape;
$\varepsilon > 0$ — hyperbolic flyby.

## The shapes of orbits

Start at distance <M v="r0">r_0</M> with purely tangential speed
<M v="vt">v_t</M>. Two special speeds organize everything:

$$
v_{circ} = \sqrt{\frac{\mu}{r_0}}, \qquad
v_{esc} = \sqrt{\frac{2\mu}{r_0}} = \sqrt{2}\;v_{circ}
$$

- $v_t = v_{circ}$: circle.
- $v_{circ} < v_t < v_{esc}$: ellipse, starting at **periapsis**.
- $v_t < v_{circ}$: ellipse, starting at **apoapsis** (you fall inward first).
- $v_t \geq v_{esc}$: escape — never returns.

And Kepler's third law falls out of the same equations:
$T^2 \propto a^3$, where $a = -\mu/2\varepsilon$ is the semi-major axis.

**Experiments to try**

1. **Circular** preset: distance graph is flat. Compare the *v for
   circle* readout with the actual speed.
2. **Elliptical**: watch the body speed up near the star and slow down
   far away — that is Kepler's second law (constant $h$) in action.
3. **Comet-like**: extreme ellipse. Where is it fastest? Compare $r_{min}$
   and $r_{max}$.
4. **Escape**: the *Energy* readout is positive and the status says
   *unbound*. Nudge $v_t$ just below $v_{esc}$ — what changes?
5. Keep an eye on the *Conservation-law drift* graph: energy drift stays
   tiny and angular momentum drift is essentially zero. That is not
   luck — the simulation uses a **symplectic integrator** (velocity
   Verlet), designed to respect conservation laws over millions of steps.
6. **Plunge**: too little tangential speed and gravity wins. Real
   satellites deorbit the same way — by slowing down, not by aiming down.

> Everything here scales: replace $\mu$ with $GM_{Earth}$ and distances
> in metres with kilometres, and you are computing real satellite orbits.
