# Energy & Momentum

Some quantities refuse to change. Two of them — momentum and energy —
turn messy problems into one-line answers.

## Two conservation laws

The **momentum** of a body is $\vec{p} = m\vec{v}$. Newton's second law
says force is the rate of change of momentum, so with no external force,
total momentum is constant:

$$
\vec{p}_{total} = \sum_i m_i \vec{v}_i = \text{const}
$$

The **kinetic energy** is $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv^2$. Unlike momentum, it is
*not* always conserved — it can hide in deformation, heat and sound. A
collision is called **elastic** when kinetic energy survives it intact.

## Collisions

For two bodies on a line, momentum conservation plus the **coefficient of
restitution** <M v="e">e</M> fully determine the outcome:

$$
e = \frac{v_2' - v_1'}{v_1 - v_2}
\qquad
\begin{cases}
e = 1 & \text{elastic} \\
0 < e < 1 & \text{partially inelastic} \\
e = 0 & \text{perfectly inelastic (bodies stick)}
\end{cases}
$$

Solving the two equations gives

$$
v_1' = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 + m_2 e (v_2 - v_1)}{m_1 + m_2},
\qquad
v_2' = \frac{m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2 + m_1 e (v_1 - v_2)}{m_1 + m_2}
$$

Watch the readouts: **momentum stays constant through every collision**,
while kinetic energy drops whenever $e < 1$.

**Experiments to try**

1. **Elastic, equal masses**: the bodies simply exchange velocities.
   Verify with $v_1 = 3$, $v_2 = -1$.
2. **Perfectly inelastic**: after impact both move at the center-of-mass
   velocity $v_{cm} = (m_1 v_1 + m_2 v_2)/(m_1+m_2)$. Compute it, then
   check the readouts. How much kinetic energy was lost?
3. **Heavy hits light** ($m_1 \gg m_2$, target at rest): show from the
   formulas that the light body leaves at almost $2v_1$. Try it.
4. **Light hits heavy**: the projectile bounces back at nearly its
   original speed. Which everyday situation is this?
5. Watch the *Energy & momentum* graph: the momentum line is flat through
   ball–ball collisions but jumps at the walls. Why? (Hint: the walls are
   external.)

> Maximum energy loss happens at $e = 0$: the kinetic energy of the
> center-of-mass motion can never be removed — momentum conservation
> forbids it.
