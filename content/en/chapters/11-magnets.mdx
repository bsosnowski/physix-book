# Magnets & Magnetic Fields

A magnet is the only object most people have held that pushes without
touching. Its reach is a **field** — and, as Oersted accidentally proved
in 1820, electric current makes the very same field.

## Poles and fields

Every magnet has two poles, N and S. Like poles repel, unlike attract —
and cutting a magnet in half gives you two magnets, never a lone pole.
(No magnetic monopole has ever been found; it is one of physics' open
questions.)

A compass needle is a tiny magnet: its north tip points along the local
field $\vec{B}$. Sprinkle compasses around a magnet and they *draw* the
field lines for you — out of N, around, into S. Inside the magnet the
lines continue S→N: magnetic field lines always close on themselves.

**Experiments to try**

1. Drag the bar magnet around the compass grid — the whole map rotates
   and bends to follow. Find the two spots where needles line up
   end-to-end with the magnet (on its axis).
2. **Reverse polarity**: every needle flips 180°. The field pattern is
   identical, the direction opposite.
3. Above the magnet's center, which way do needles point? Explain using
   "out of N, into S".

## Oersted's discovery

Switch the mode to a **wire** (current perpendicular to the screen).
The compasses arrange into *circles* — a magnetic field with no poles at
all, wrapping the current:

$$
B = \frac{\mu_0 I}{2\pi r}
$$

The direction follows the **right-hand rule**: thumb along the current,
fingers curl along B. Current out of the screen (⊙) → counterclockwise.

**Experiments:** flip the current (⊗) and watch every needle reverse;
double the current — same circles, stiffer alignment (stronger field);
drag the wire and the whole vortex follows. This one observation —
a compass twitching near a battery wire — unified electricity and
magnetism and led, within a decade, to the electromagnet and the motor.

<Exercise title="Which way does the needle point?">
A wire carries current vertically upward. A compass sits directly east
of the wire. Which way does its north tip point?

<Solution>
Right hand: thumb up (current), fingers curl — on the east side they
point **north** (horizontal, perpendicular to the wire–compass line).
Oersted's twitch: the needle deflects 90° from the wire, not toward it.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise title="Cutting a magnet">
You cut a bar magnet in half, hoping to isolate the N pole. What do you
get, and why does the compass map of each half look like a full
magnet's?

<Solution>
Two complete magnets, each with N and S. Magnetism comes from electron
spin/orbital currents — atomic-scale current loops, and every loop is a
dipole. No arrangement of loops yields a single pole; the field lines
must always close.
</Solution>
</Exercise>
