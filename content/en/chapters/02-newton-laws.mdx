# Newton's Laws of Motion

Kinematics describes motion; dynamics explains it. The bridge between the
two is **force**.

## The three laws

1. **Inertia.** A body remains at rest or in uniform motion unless acted
   upon by a net external force.
2. **$\vec{F} = m\vec{a}$.** The net force on a body equals its mass times
   its acceleration:

$$
\sum \vec{F} = m \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}
$$

3. **Action–reaction.** If body A exerts a force $\vec{F}_{AB}$ on body B,
   then B exerts $-\vec{F}_{AB}$ on A.

The second law is the engine of almost every simulation in this book: give
us the forces, and we can integrate the motion forward in time.

## The inclined plane

Place a block of mass <M v="m">m</M> on a ramp tilted at
<M v="angle">\alpha</M>. Three forces act on it: gravity $m\vec{g}$, the
normal force $N$, and friction $f$. Splitting gravity into components
parallel and perpendicular to the slope:

$$
F_\parallel = mg\sin\alpha, \qquad N = mg\cos\alpha
$$

**Static friction** can supply at most $f_{max} = \mu N$, so the block
starts to slide only when

$$
mg\sin\alpha > \mu\, mg\cos\alpha
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\tan\alpha > \mu
$$

Notice the mass cancels: whether the block slides depends only on the
angle and the friction coefficient. The critical angle is
$\alpha_c = \arctan\mu$. Once sliding, the acceleration is

$$
a = g\left(\sin\alpha - \mu\cos\alpha\right)
$$

**Experiments to try**

1. Start with the **Sticky** preset. The block holds still. Slowly raise
   the angle slider — at what angle does it break free? Compare with the
   *Critical angle* readout, $\arctan\mu$.
2. Set the **On the verge** preset ($\mu = 0{,}58$, $\alpha = 30^\circ$).
   Nudge the angle up by one degree, then down by one. Static friction is
   a threshold, not a gradual effect.
3. While the block slides, change its **mass**. Does the acceleration
   change? Explain using the formula above.
4. Let the block pick up speed on a steep slope, then flatten the ramp
   live. Friction decelerates it — and it stops *without sliding back*.
   Why can friction never push it uphill?
5. Use the **Step** button while paused to inspect the motion one
   physics tick (1/120 s) at a time.

> The velocity graph is a straight line while sliding — constant
> acceleration is the fingerprint of constant net force.
