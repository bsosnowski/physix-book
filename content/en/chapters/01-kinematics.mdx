# Kinematics — the Geometry of Motion

Before we ask *why* things move, we need a precise language for *how* they
move. Kinematics is that language: position, velocity and acceleration,
without any mention of forces.

## Describing motion

The position of a point on a plane is a vector $\vec{r}(t) = (x(t),\, y(t))$.
Velocity is its rate of change, and acceleration is the rate of change of
velocity:

$$
\vec{v} = \frac{d\vec{r}}{dt}, \qquad \vec{a} = \frac{d\vec{v}}{dt}
$$

When acceleration is constant, integrating twice gives the familiar
equations of motion:

$$
\vec{r}(t) = \vec{r}_0 + \vec{v}_0\, t + \tfrac{1}{2}\vec{a}\, t^2
$$

## Projectile motion

Throw a ball with speed <M v="v0">v_0</M> at an angle <M v="angle">\theta</M>
above the horizontal. Ignoring air resistance, the only acceleration is
gravity, $a_y = -g$, so the horizontal and vertical motions are independent:

*(Hover a symbol like <M v="angle">\theta</M> — the matching slider below
lights up, and vice versa.)*

$$
x(t) = v_0 \cos\theta \cdot t, \qquad
y(t) = v_0 \sin\theta \cdot t - \tfrac{1}{2} g t^2
$$

The trajectory is a parabola. The range on level ground is

$$
R = \frac{v_0^2 \sin 2\theta}{g}
$$
