# The Electromagnetic Spectrum

Light is an electromagnetic wave — oscillating electric and magnetic
fields carrying themselves through empty space at

$$
c = 3\times10^8\ \text{m/s}, \qquad c = \lambda f
$$

Radio, microwaves, infrared, the colours you see, ultraviolet, X-rays
and gamma rays are **the same phenomenon** — differing only in
frequency. Nineteen orders of magnitude of one wave.

**Experiments to try**

1. **FM radio**: λ ≈ 3 m — that is why rooftop antennas are metres
   long. An efficient antenna is a sizeable fraction of λ.
2. **Wi-Fi**: λ ≈ 12.5 cm — the antenna hides inside your laptop lid.
   Why does 5 GHz Wi-Fi struggle through walls more than 2.4 GHz?
3. **Green light**: λ ≈ 550 nm — a thousand times smaller than a
   bacterium. No antenna; atoms and molecules do the emitting.
4. Sweep slowly through the **visible** sliver: the wave changes colour
   exactly across 400–790 THz. Our entire visual world is one octave of
   a 19-decade piano.

## Photons and danger

Energy arrives in packets — photons — of size

$$
E = h f
$$

This single equation explains the safety labels. A radio photon carries
~10⁻⁶ eV: a trillion of them cannot break a chemical bond. A UV photon
carries several eV — enough to damage DNA (sunburn). X-ray and gamma
photons carry keV–MeV: they ionize atoms outright.

**Intensity is not the issue — frequency is.** A blazing red lamp will
never give you sunburn; faint UV will. That is the quantum lesson
hiding inside a classical spectrum.

<Exercise title="Your microwave's wavelength">
A microwave oven runs at 2.45 GHz. Compute λ. The rotating plate and
the metal mesh in the door both relate to this number — how?

<Solution>
$\lambda = c/f = 3\times10^8 / 2.45\times10^9 \approx 12.2$ cm. Standing
waves in the cavity create hot/cold spots ~6 cm apart — the turntable
moves food through them. The door mesh holes (~2 mm) are far smaller
than λ, so microwaves cannot pass, while visible light (λ ~ 0.0005 mm)
streams through: you can watch, but not cook your face.
</Solution>
</Exercise>

<Exercise title="Photon budget">
A 1 W green laser (λ = 550 nm) emits how many photons per second?
Compare with a 1 W FM transmitter at 100 MHz.

<Solution>
$E_{photon} = hc/\lambda \approx 3.6\times10^{-19}$ J → about
$2.8\times10^{18}$ photons/s. The FM photon is ~$6.6\times10^{-26}$ J →
$1.5\times10^{25}$ photons/s — ten million times more, each ten million
times weaker. Same power, radically different granularity.
</Solution>
</Exercise>
